# Q598 source and priority assessment

**Verdict, 27 November 2026.** A bounded primary-source search found no earlier matching finite-sample minimax expected Euclidean risk across both regimes of the parameter-dependent Gaussian pair model. The original paper establishes the fixed-dimension acceleration, the linear comparison representation, and the feasibility estimator. A related dimension exponent already occurs for Poisson hyperplanes. Confidence in the bounded search is moderate.

## Exact source and comparison design

Marc Jourdan, Gizem Yüce and Nicolas Flammarion, *Learning Parametric Distributions from Samples and Preferences*, ICML 2025, PMLR 267, 28418–28445. Section 2 observes independent triples ((X_i,Y_i,Z_i)), with both points in each fresh pair drawn independently from the unknown (p_	heta). For deterministic preferences and reward (r_	heta=log p_	heta), the label is the sign of (log p_	heta(X_i)-log p_	heta(Y_i)). Both points are observed. For known identity covariance this is nearest-point preference from the Gaussian mean, with null ties. The comparison design is passive and depends on the parameter through the sampling law. This differs from queries chosen by the learner, observations with reused items, or a Gaussian design known in advance and independent of the ideal point. [Published proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/jourdan25a.html) · [Author version §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23557v1#S2) · [Pinned catalog Q598](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/mathematical-statistics.md#q598).

Theorem 4.8 and §4.1 control the entire version space by a directional deviation bound and a covering number. Section 5 asks to close the dimension gap after Theorem 5.3 (proceedings PDF p. 8), which measures expected Euclidean error using the entire triple law. Appendix F and Lemma F.1 (proceedings PDF p. 20) give the Gaussian directional coefficient through the reciprocal of the spherical first absolute moment, of order (sqrt d) at identity covariance. Thus the source supplies a lower bound (Omega(min{sqrt{d/n},d/n})) and an upper bound of order (d^{3/2}/n) up to logarithms in the large-sample Gaussian regime. In particular, the source's (1/n) description at fixed dimension already holds. With a full cube of means the present result gets a lower order (d^{3/2}/n) in the fast regime, removes the logarithmic loss in the upper bound, and gives the crossover at (n) of order (d^2). The cube is a full-dimensional, translation-equivalent parameter choice to a fixed side-length cube as in the source's discussion. No claim is made for every bounded parameter set in the general source model. [Author version §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23557v1#S4.SS1) · [§5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23557v1#S5) · [Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23557v1#A6).

## Closest primary work

- Andrew K. Massimino and Mark A. Davenport, *As You Like It: Localization via Paired Comparisons*, JMLR 22(186), 1–39 (2021). Theorem 1 uses Gaussian items centered at a known zero independently of the unknown ideal point, with design variance (2R^2/d) for a radius-(R) parameter ball. Its uniform tessellation bound has a logarithmic factor. Theorem 3 is a deterministic worst-cell bound by counting regions of a hyperplane arrangement. These are related comparison geometry and scaling results. They supply neither the Gaussian pair information nor the present two-regime minimax lower bound under the parameter-dependent sample law. [Paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/18-105.html).
- François Baccelli and Eliza O'Reilly, *The stochastic geometry of unconstrained one-bit data compression*, Electron. J. Probab. 24 (2019), DOI 10.1214/19-EJP389, §3.4 and Table 4, study the circumradius of the zero cell of a stationary isotropic Poisson hyperplane tessellation and identify the (d^{3/2})-over-intensity scale. That exponent and the comparison-boundary geometry therefore have prior stochastic-geometric counterparts. The model there is a Poisson hyperplane process and the cited scale is high-dimensional, with a different statistical target. Dirksen and Mendelson's *Non-Gaussian hyperplane tessellations and robust one-bit compressed sensing*, JEMS 23, 2913–2947 (2021), use parameter-independent random hyperplanes with uniform shifts to control uniform distances and reconstruction. The cap-hitting step here is a classical VC epsilon-net argument of Haussler and Welzl applied after restricting attention to small offsets. The manuscript supplies its finite-sample argument. [Baccelli and O'Reilly](https://doi.org/10.1214/19-EJP389) · [Dirksen and Mendelson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09409) · [Haussler and Welzl](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02187876).
- Marc Jourdan and Nicolas Flammarion, *Learning Distributions from Samples and Qualities*, arXiv:2610.17956, submitted 13 October 2026, observe each Gaussian draw with one thresholded quality label. Their §§1.2 and 4 explicitly distinguish independent pair labels from comparisons derived from a shared pool of observations. Their §5 and Appendix B.4 discuss the dimension gap for thresholded rewards in their own model. These results do not give the sharp rate for the independent-pair experiment. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17956).

## Contribution

The lower bound separates the Gaussian pair information from the conditional entropy of deterministic comparison labels. Averaging logistic log loss under the hypercube prior avoids paying a worst-direction label cost. The upper bound applies simultaneous constant-mass cap coverage to the random set of near-boundary observations. The selection uses the unknown mean only in the analysis. The estimator uses all observed comparison halfspaces. The result gives absolute comparison constants in the expected Euclidean norm for all (d,nge1) on the fixed cube. No sharp numerical constants or general claims about other preference designs are asserted.

The search used the exact published open question, author and proceedings pages, newer citing work, paired-comparison localization, Gaussian hyperplane tessellations and minimax rate terminology. No absolute priority claim is made.
